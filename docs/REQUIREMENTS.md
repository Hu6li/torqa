# ORQWA — Requirements

Outcome of the initial requirements-elicitation session (2026-10-02, ~70 questions), extended
2026-10-03 with course previews, start page and realistic graphics (R36–R47) and in-ride
settings, ride names, per-rider HUD layouts, UI quality and direct HUD placement (R48–R54),
plus overlay mode and constant-power / heart-rate workouts (R55–R56), refined 2026-10-06: an
overlay over normal windows only, a Workouts tab and Di2 shifting (R57–R59), extended 2026-10-12
with uploads by one click or automatically (R60) and the analysis of a rider's power across
their rides (R61–R65, #217), and 2026-10-13 with an estimated time to the finish (R66, #231)
and a searchable, sortable and grouped course gallery with the countries courses pass
(R67–R68, #230), and 2026-10-14 with the minimap's land around the whole route (R70, #236)
and power meters and the cadence's source (R71–R72) and a ride chart with cadence and lines to
hide (R73).
Requirement IDs (`R<n>`) are referenced from code, tests and ADRs. The list is **append-only**:
existing requirements are never reworded or renumbered; changes are new requirements that state
what they supersede, and the old entry only gets a short pointer.

## Vision

ORQWA is a modern, **offline-first**, open-source indoor cycling app. Import a GPX route and ride it
on a smart trainer (primary: Wahoo KICKR Core 2) through a generated semi-realistic 3D world
*(stylized since 2026-10-05, R44)*, a synced ride video, or street-level imagery.

## General

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R1 | macOS (Apple Silicon M1+) first; Windows and Linux later. Stack must be cross-platform from day one. |
| R2 | Audience: the author + friends. Open source under **GPL-3.0**. **Zero budget**: free/open data only; users bring their own API keys for paid services. |
| R3 | Offline-first: a prepared ride works without internet. Online extras (DEM correction, world data download, Street View, uploads) are used when connected. |
| R4 | Extensibility is a core principle: interface-driven plugins for route importers, devices, shift inputs, uploaders, ride modes and workout parsers. |
| R99 | Extends R1 (2026-10-19): on **Windows** ORQWA comes with an **installer** (`.msi`): it installs into Program Files with a Start-menu entry, is removed under Installed apps, and a newer installer updates it in place. Installing, updating and uninstalling keep the rider's data. ORQWA will then **look for updates** itself, once at start-up and never during a ride, behind a setting that turns it off, and install a new version when the rider agrees (ADR 0023). |
| R100 | Extends R1 (2026-10-19): ORQWA runs on **Android tablets** as an APK from GitHub Releases, with the trainer, sensors and shifter over Bluetooth as on a computer; phones run it with low graphics. Android support stays removable: it lives apart from the desktop build (ADR 0024). |

## Devices

|---|---|

| R6 | Direct-drive trainers first (KICKR Core 2). Architecture generic enough for any FTMS/FE-C trainer and smart bikes. |
| R7 | Sensors: BLE heart-rate strap. Zwift Click as shift input (no official protocol → reverse-engineered BLE, isolated and optional); also OpenBikeControl and keyboard/gamepad as shift inputs. *(Shift inputs: R59; power meters: R71; cadence: R72.)* |
| R8 | Trainer modes: **SIM** (slope simulation, MVP), **ERG** (target power), **resistance level**; free ride. |
| R9 | Drivetrain: real cassette (trainer handles shifting naturally) and Zwift Cog (app-side **virtual gears** — resistance offset via FTMS). Post-MVP. See ADR 0003. |
| R10 | Software **fake trainer** for development and automated tests. |
| R71 | Extends R7 (2026-10-14): a Bluetooth **power meter** (Cycling Power Service — power pedals such as the Favero Assioma, crank and spider meters) beside the trainer. While it reports, **its power is the rider's**: for the speed, the HUD, the recording and the analysis; its **cadence** too where it reports one (worked out from its crank revolutions), else the trainer's *(choice of source: R72)*. If it drops, the trainer's power and cadence count until it is back, and if the trainer drops the meter's power still moves the rider. It is chosen in Devices & Settings, remembered and reconnected like the other devices (R41, R69). The trainer still holds ERG targets by its own measurement; matching them to the meter is a later step *(matching dropped 2026-10-15)*. |
| R72 | Extends R71 (2026-10-14): the rider chooses **where the cadence comes from**, as Zwift's pairing screen does: **Automatic** (a cadence sensor if one is connected, else the power meter, else the trainer), the **trainer**, the **power meter**, or a Bluetooth **cadence sensor** (Cycling Speed and Cadence Service). While the chosen device reports no cadence, the others stand in, in the automatic order. The choice is kept for the installation; a chosen sensor is remembered and reconnected like the other devices. |
| R110 | Extends R5 and R6 (2026-10-21): the **Tacx NEO 2 Smart** can connect as a controllable trainer through **Tacx FE-C over Bluetooth**, with power, cadence and speed readings, slope simulation (grade, wind and rolling resistance), ERG target power and resistance. Discovery chooses a trainer before its power-meter service; FTMS takes precedence on devices offering both protocols. The existing trainer selection, remembered devices and reconnection apply. No ANT+ radio or dongle is required. The protocol implementation is tested without hardware. |

## Routes

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R11 | Import **GPX** (MVP); importer interface allows TCX/FIT/KML later. Routes are used as-is (no route editor). |
| R12 | Elevation: when online, correct with a terrain model (Mapterhorn, AWS Terrain Tiles fallback — ADR 0005) and smooth; offline, use cached tiles or GPX elevation with smoothing. |
| R126 | Extends R11 (2026-10-24): **roundabouts are ridden the way traffic goes round them**: counter-clockwise where traffic keeps right, clockwise where it keeps left, also where a planner's file has no points on the ring and the way round is the long one. A point recorded a few metres back round the ring is GPS wander, never an extra lap; a turning loop at the end of a street is ridden as the file goes. Kept worlds offer regeneration (R101). |
| R143 | Extends R126 (2026-10-25): **roundabouts as the map detail maps them** ([ADR 0033](adr/0033-map-detail.md)): where a course is prepared with a region's map detail (R142), the roads going round a roundabout are those OpenStreetMap marks as one, ridden the way it draws them — also where the map tiles join a ring to a road leading off it or draw it the other way round, and where a ring is too small, too large or too long for its shape to tell. Without the detail, roundabouts are found as R126 finds them. |
| R145 | Refines R126 and R143 (2026-10-25): **once round a roundabout per visit**: the line leaves a roundabout the first time it passes the way out the file takes, also where points of the file lie beside the ring past that way out — as where the file follows a path beside the roundabout, or rests there — rather than going round again to reach them. Turning back at a roundabout goes round it once. Kept worlds offer regeneration (R101). |

## Training & UX

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R21 | Structured workouts later, but architected for now: ZWO, ERG/MRC, FIT workouts, built-in editor. No multi-week training plans. *(Constant-power and heart-rate workouts: R56.)* |
| R22 | Rider profile: weight, FTP, max HR, power and HR zones, built-in FTP test. **Multiple user profiles** per installation. |
| R23 | Fully customizable HUD widgets: power (instant/3 s/10 s), cadence, HR, speed, distance, time, elevation gain, current & upcoming gradient, W/kg, NP, TSS, kJ, zone. *(Per-rider layout and editor: R51. Estimated time to go: R66.)* |
| R24 | Metric and imperial units. English UI first, i18n-ready from day one. |
| R25 | Single window / fullscreen, external monitor / TV. Phone companion app is a possible future extension. *(Overlay mode: R55. A remote in the browser of a phone or tablet instead of an app: R86.)* |
| R26 | Audio, selectable per ride: ambient sounds, original video audio, music app control. |
| R114 | Extends R26 (2026-10-22, #259): 3D course rides have **convincing wind and bicycle sound**, available offline. Wind varies continuously with speed and irregular gusts rather than repeating a sound track; weather affects its breeze. Chain sound follows cadence and effort, and a separate freewheel follows road speed while coasting. Wind and bicycle volume can each be changed or muted before and during a ride. Pause, finish, abort and leaving the ride stop playback; video rides keep their original audio and workouts without a course stay quiet. Bundled recordings have redistributable licences and source credits. |
| R116 | Extends R114 (2026-10-22): each successful **virtual gear change** in a live 3D course ride plays a short, recorded mechanical shifting sound through the bicycle volume control, for keyboard, BLE shifters and the phone remote alike. Gear-limit presses and selecting the current gear are silent. Rapid shifts may overlap without building a playback backlog; pause, finish and leaving the ride discard pending shifts. Shifts while paused, before the trainer connects, on video rides or workouts without a course are not queued for later. Physical cassette changes are not reported by the trainer and do not trigger an invented shift. |
| R117 | Refines R116 (2026-10-22): shifting includes the **chain moving between gears and settling**, using recorded chain transfers with variation, rather than only the lever click. Chain movement follows cadence and effort and blends back into the continuous drivetrain sound. While coasting, the lever acts immediately but the chain transfer waits for pedaling; stopping pedaling during a transfer stops its chain movement until pedaling resumes. Bicycle volume and the existing pause/finish/leave rules apply to both parts. |
| R153 | Extends R26 and R114 (2026-10-27, #377): the **Rain weather preset** adds continuous rain sound to a 3D course ride, available offline and audible while stationary or coasting. Switching weather fades the rain in or out; the existing **Wind sound** volume controls and mutes it before and during the ride. Pause, finish, abort and leaving the ride stop it with the other riding sound. Video rides keep their original audio, and workouts without a course stay quiet. Bundled recordings have redistributable licences and source credits. |
| R27 | Auto-detected climbs with personal KOM-style times; personal records per route. Multiplayer possibly later — keep simulation state separable from rendering. |
| R66 | Extends R23 and R40 (2026-10-13, #231): an **estimated time to the finish**. The course page shows how long the route takes the rider at 75 % of their FTP, with their weight and the descents as chosen in the ride options; the HUD can show the **time to go**, which follows the rider's average power so far (moving over to it from 75 % of FTP during the first ten minutes). Both ride the rest of the route with the ride's own physics, so climbs and descents count as they will. *(Shown to the nearest 5 minutes: R67.)* |
| R124 | Extends R27 and R42 (2026-10-24, #185): the ride tells the rider of its **moments**: a climb topped (a personal record when faster than ever), a new best power over 5 s, 1, 5, 20 or 60 minutes (told once the effort tops out, for riders with earlier records), halfway, 1 km to go and every 25 km (1 mile and 25 miles in imperial units), a hard step of a structured workout held on its target (from a minute at 75 % of FTP, its average power within 5 % of the target, ridden to its end), and the first ride on a course. Each shows for a few seconds as a card at the top centre over the HUD, its figure large and a line below, one after the other; the overlay shows the line in its bar. Every rider picks a **voice** in their profile: **Casual** (default, warm and cheering, a line picked at random from a few), **Plain** (the facts) or **Off** (no cards). Lines are written in full sentences, avoid slang that ages and are kept together in one table; translations follow the same voices. The finish is the summary's to tell. Both summaries (R113, R42) add at most **one line**, in the rider's voice (plain when off) and looking back on the ride, for what their figures and record badges do not say: the first ride on the course, or else the rider's week from Monday in local time, the rides so far or against an optional number of **rides planned per week** in the profile. Nothing is said of a lone ride without a plan. ORQWA still plans no training (R21). Simulated rides set no records and count towards no week. |
| R125 | Extends R124 (2026-10-24, #185): a moment's card arrives with a short **burst of particles** in the brand blue and the power zones' colours and a short **chime**, also when the overlay shows the line. A **Sound** setting for the installation turns the chime off; it is on by default. The chime is made by ORQWA itself, needs no download and plays on every kind of ride, independent of the per-ride wind, bicycle and video sound (R26, R114). |

## Courses

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R32 | **Course files** (`.tqc`): a prepared course is saved as one self-contained file that can be stored, copied, shared (mail, Nextcloud, USB, websites) and imported again, fully offline. See ADR 0007. |
| R33 | A course contains the original GPX, the processed route (elevations, bridges/tunnels), the corridor's terrain heights and OpenStreetMap features, the **pre-built 3D world**, a preview image and a manifest (format and generator versions, name, stats, attribution). |
| R34 | **Course library**: a `courses/` folder in the data directory (may be synced, R30). The app lists its courses to pick from, imports `.tqc` files into it and saves newly prepared routes as courses. |
| R35 | Courses built by an older generator still ride instantly from their stored world; they can be rebuilt from the stored inputs with a newer generator. Video courses (R17) use the same format and reference their video file instead of embedding it. |
| R36 | **Course screenshots**: every course carries a small screenshot gallery stored in the `.tqc`. 3D courses render it automatically from the generated world when the course is built (e.g. start, highest point, scenic spots); video courses take frames from the video at fixed distances. The user can pick which image is the **cover**. *(dropped 2026-10-15: the path card, R37, is the course's picture.)* |
| R37 | **Path card**: every course also has a route image: the GPX path in the logo blue **`#2EB0FF`** with start/finish markers, drawn on the course's own map (#192): the land around the whole route in black and greys with its waters, main roads and the names of its towns (#203), kept in the course file as a picture when the course is prepared; or on black for a course without a place. The elevation profile is shown on the course detail page only (review of #28). |
| R95 | Extends R34 (2026-10-19): a **new library starts with a sample course**, Twann – Chasseral (20.4 km and 1,190 m of climbing from Lake Biel up to the Chasseral), shipped with the app and prepared with its terrain and map, so that a rider who has just downloaded ORQWA can ride at once, offline. It is added on the first start, when the data directory has no library yet; an existing library stays as it is, so riders updating ORQWA get no sample, and a deleted sample does not come back. |
| R101 | Extends R34 and R78, and supersedes R35's stored world (2026-10-20, #237): a setting of this computer, **Keep courses ready to ride**, keeps a library course's 3D world once it is built — when the course is prepared from a GPX file or first ridden — so that **Ride** on it later starts within a second or two instead of waiting for the world to be built (about half a minute for a 100 km course on a fast computer, longer on others). It is **off until turned on**, as a world takes about 10 MB per kilometre of route; the settings tell how much room the kept worlds take. They are kept in the **cache directory**, never in the course file (which stays as cheap to share) nor in the data directory (which may sync, R30). Kept worlds **stay across updates**: a world an older ORQWA kept is ridden as it is, and when an update changes how worlds look, the course's page offers to **Regenerate world**, building and keeping it anew; one an update can no longer read or draw is built again on the next ride. A course's world is found by what it is built from — its track and data files — so renaming it or finding its countries keeps it; a damaged kept world is built again without bothering the rider. Deleting a course deletes its world, also when another computer sharing the library deletes it; turning the setting off deletes them all, and turning it on keeps the world of the course loaded at once (ADR 0025). |
| R136 | Extends R101 (2026-10-24, #355): while courses are kept ready to ride, the settings' Courses tab **lists each kept world** by its course's name with the room it takes on disk, in the MB and GB riders know from their drives (powers of 1000, not MiB or GiB), and a bin beside it **deletes that world alone**: the course stays in the library and its next ride builds and keeps the world again, without asking, as nothing but time is lost. A world two courses share (the same course in the library twice) is listed once under both names and deleted for both. A last row tells the total the kept worlds take, in place of the note's sentence before. |
| R142 | Extends R119 and R101 (2026-10-25): **road surfaces from map detail** ([ADR 0033](adr/0033-map-detail.md)): off until the rider turns on **Download map detail** in the settings' Courses tab on this computer. The rider adds regions from Geofabrik's list (countries and the parts of large ones), seeing a region's download size first; ORQWA downloads a region only when asked, keeps it in the cache and lists it with the date of its data and the room it takes, to **download again** (telling an unchanged one up to date) or **delete**. A download stopped goes on where it stopped; turning the setting off asks, then deletes every region. Downloads already in the cache without their detail are prepared once the setting is on. Courses prepared afterwards give each road the surface the region maps where the map tiles tell only paved or unpaved — cobbles, paving, concrete, dirt, grass — carry that detail in their course file and come out the same wherever they are opened; courses prepared before stay as they were. Preparing, opening, riding and regenerating a course never download it. (Refined by R144.) |
| R144 | Refines R142 (2026-10-25): **courses already in the library take map detail too**: a library course opened while a region's map detail is ready on this computer reads it — also in place of an older download of that region it carries — and its course file is brought up to date with that detail and with the route matched with it; its kept world is built again on its next ride (R101), with no course to delete or import again. A course keeps the detail it carries of regions not downloaded here; one opened with no region ready, or from outside the library, stays as it was. Supersedes R142's "courses prepared before stay as they were". |

Sharing is file-based for now; a built-in online catalog may follow later.

## App structure & ride flow

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R38 | A real **start page** separate from the ride view, with a top tab bar: **Courses**, **History**, **Profile**, **Devices & Settings**. The ride view only shows the ride. *(Workouts tab: R58; Analysis tab: R61.)* |
| R39 | **Courses tab**: gallery overview of the course library (R34) — each card shows the cover or path card, distance, elevation gain, a small map, the course type (3D / video) and other key stats. Importing a GPX / preparing a new course starts here. *(Search, sort, filters and groups: R67.)* |
| R40 | **Course detail page** (opened from a card): screenshot gallery *(dropped 2026-10-15, with R36)*, path card, map, elevation profile, stats, personal records on the course, and the per-ride options (difficulty, descent mode, weather, time of day, camera, ghost) → **Ride**. |
| R41 | The last-used trainer and sensors **reconnect automatically** in the background at app start; the user is only prompted if that fails when a ride starts. |
| R42 | At the end of a ride a **summary screen** (stats, charts, PRs, save/discard, upload) is shown, then the app returns to the start page. *(Quick dialog: R113.)* |
| R48 | **In-ride settings dialog**: the ride view's controls (today's bottom buttons: camera, HUD customization) move into one settings dialog opened from a single button or key. It is shared: the same dialog and settings (camera, HUD, difficulty, descent mode, weather, time of day, …) are used from the course detail page (R40), so options are set the same way before and during a ride. |
| R49 | The in-ride dialog also ends the ride: **finish and save** (→ summary, R42) or **abort without saving**, the latter after a confirmation. |
| R50 | **Ride names**: every ride in the history has a name, defaulting to the course name and date. It can be set on the summary screen (R42) and renamed later in the history; it is used as the activity name in FIT export and uploads. Files on disk keep stable identifiers, so renaming is sync-safe (ADR 0002). |
| R51 | **HUD per rider**: each rider profile has its own HUD layout (R23), editable in the rider's profile settings. The editor shows a separate HUD preview, and widgets are added, removed and **reordered by drag and drop**. It is the same editor as the HUD section of the in-ride settings dialog (R48), so changes made during a ride are saved to the rider's layout. *(Placement refined by R54.)* |
| R52 | **Modern, coherent UI** in a *sleek minimal* style: slim typography, thin translucent panels, generous spacing, few controls visible at once; during a ride the 3D view dominates. All screens and dialogs share one design system (type scale, spacing, colours, corner radii, control styles); no unstyled stock widgets. Course summaries (course cards, detail page, the loaded-course panel) are designed as polished cards with a clear hierarchy of name, path card (R37), key stats and elevation profile. |
| R53 | **Stable, responsive layouts**: loading a course or other content never changes font sizes or lets the layout grow or jump. Resizable windows and dialogs (e.g. rider settings) reflow and resize their inputs with the window instead of leaving them fixed in the middle. Layouts work from 1280×720 up to 4K / TV (R25). *(The interface keeps its size and the layouts reflow, down to 1024 × 640: R94.)* |
| R54 | Refines R51: HUD widgets are arranged **directly in the HUD preview**, not only in a list. A widget is dragged from the widget list straight to the position where it should appear, moved around within the HUD, and dragged out to remove it; a drop-position indicator shows where it will land. |
| R179 | Extends R23 and R54 (2026-11-01, #432): every HUD figure about power or heart rate has a **thin bar in its zone's colour down its left edge**, where the drop-position line runs while editing. Each figure shows the zone of its **own value**: power, 3 s and 10 s power, average and normalized power in the rider's power zones by their own watts, W/kg, % of FTP and the power zone by the live power; heart rate, % of maximum and the heart-rate zone in the heart-rate zones. Without a value, or for a rider without an FTP or maximum heart rate, there is no bar. The HUD editor shows example bars, in the overlay too. The zones are worked out in the core; the app only colours them. |
| R182 | Refines R179 (2026-11-01, #432): the **large figure has no zone bar**; under a large power figure, its line of W/kg, % of FTP and zone is coloured by the zone already. A **large heart rate** gets such a line too: its **% of the maximum and heart-rate zone**, coloured by that zone, none for a rider without a maximum. The figures below the large one keep their bars. |
| R113 | Refines R42 (2026-10-22): finishing a route normally opens a **quick summary dialog automatically**, over the finished ride, styled like the ride settings. **Finish & save** opens the same dialog. It shows time, distance, climbing and the recorded average power, heart rate and cadence, in the rider's units; missing sensor figures are omitted.
